Why This Exists

You deserve
great healthcare.
Not just good.

Most people navigate their health completely alone — leaving appointments confused, managing conditions without real guidance, and making important decisions without someone in their corner. Mobile Health Nurse exists to change that.

The reality

What's actually happening in healthcare.

The American healthcare system is built for volume. Physicians are rushed, appointment windows are short, and the language used is clinical — not conversational. Most people leave their appointments with more questions than answers.

  • The average primary care visit is 15–18 minutes. That's not enough time to understand a new diagnosis, review a medication change, and ask every question you have.
  • Confusing test results arrive in a patient portal with no context and no one to call. Most people turn to Google.
  • Transitions between specialists, hospitals, and home care are poorly coordinated. Things fall through the cracks — and nobody is responsible for the gaps.
  • Patients who don't advocate loudly for themselves often receive less thorough care — not because providers don't care, but because the system doesn't create space for it.
You are not a number. You are a person with a history, a life, and goals worth protecting.

This isn't about blaming the system or the people in it. It's about acknowledging what the system cannot do — and filling that gap with something real.

Why private pay

When I work for you — not your insurance company — everything changes.

Insurance-based care isn't designed around your needs. It's designed around what's billable, what's covered, and what can be done in the least amount of time. Private pay removes all of that.

Who I am

I built this because I watched what happens when people don't have support.

I'm Gretchen Hess — a registered nurse, ACE-certified personal trainer, and competitive triathlete. I spent years working in ICU environments, including through the COVID-19 pandemic, watching patients and families navigate their most vulnerable moments with almost no support outside the clinical walls.

I watched people leave the hospital confused. I watched families make decisions without understanding the options. I watched conditions worsen because nobody caught the early signs. I started Mobile Health Nurse because I know exactly what people need — and I know the system can't give it to them.

I also live the active aging lifestyle my clients aspire to. I'm 54, take zero medications, and am currently training for a triathlon. I don't just know what healthy looks like on paper — I know what it takes to build and maintain it at every stage of life.
